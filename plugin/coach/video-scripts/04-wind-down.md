# Video 4 — "Wind down" (about 2 minutes)

**On screen:** typing "wind down" at the end of a job; Claude updating the docs files.

When a job is done, before you close the chat, type **wind down**. It has to be the first words
of your message.

Here's what happens. Claude goes through what actually got done, checking the real thing, not
its memory. It updates your notes: what's finished, what's still open, what's waiting on you.
It writes a short log entry for the next chat. It cleans up after itself: closes the browser tabs
it opened, stops anything it left running. And if it's a coding folder, it saves the work into
the main version.

*(Show the STATUS and BACKLOG files.)* This is why tomorrow's chat knows what you did today.
Skip the wind down, and the next chat might redo work that's already finished, or ask you the
same questions again. I learned that the hard way.

Notice it writes the notes *first*, before anything else. That order matters. The notes are the
real result of the wind down.

Quick tip: it only works when your message *starts* with "wind down." If you just mention it in
a sentence, nothing happens. That's on purpose.

At the end of your next job: **wind down.**
